\chapter{Conclusion}\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Achievements}
This project delivered a working implementation of Veritas: a refinement-typed imperative source language, a type-preserving compiler, an independent DTAL verifier, and native hosted and bare-metal execution targets. The core architectural achievement is that important source-level guarantees are preserved below the frontend. Refinement constraints, contracts, array-bounds obligations, and borrowing/ownership information are carried through TAST, TIR, and DTAL, then re-checked independently at the low level rather than trusted after compilation.

The evaluation provides evidence that this design works for the implemented fragment. The frontend and independent physical-DTAL verifier agree on all 28 valid feature-suite programs, the negative suite rejects 35 invalid source programs spanning the main supported error classes, and the DTAL tampering corpus shows that representative low-level corruptions are detected after compilation. Taken together, these results support the dissertation's main claim: Veritas demonstrates that a useful SMT-verified imperative fragment can be compiled to low-level code while retaining an explicit and independently checkable safety story.

\section{Future work}
The most natural next step is to extend the supported fragment without weakening the trust architecture. This includes a richer borrowing model with non-lexical lifetimes and reborrowing, more expressive data structures and heap reasoning, and more type-directed optimisation that can exploit proven facts such as bounds or singleton information. On the assurance side, the strongest longer-term direction is to reduce trust further by formalising the verifier or its key metatheory in a proof assistant, while on the engineering side the project would benefit from broader benchmarking, larger verification corpora, and a public open-source release to make the toolchain easier to evaluate and build on.
